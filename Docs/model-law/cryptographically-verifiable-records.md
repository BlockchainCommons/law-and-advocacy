# [Draft] Cryptographically Verifiable Records Act
*(Prepared in the style of a Uniform Law Commission model law)*

* version: 2025-09-02

---

## SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
This [act] may be cited as the **Cryptographically Verifiable Records Act**.

---

## SECTION 2. DEFINITIONS.
In this [act]:

(1) **“Verifiable record system”** means any system that uses cryptographic methods to establish the authenticity, integrity, and chronological order of digital records, whether distributed, decentralized, centralized, or otherwise, including but not limited to blockchain technologies.  

(2) **“Verifiable record technology”** means computer software, hardware, or both, that enable or utilize a verifiable record system.  

(3) **“Digital record”** means information stored in electronic form, including data, documents, contracts, or communications.  

---

## SECTION 3. SELF-AUTHENTICATION OF RECORDS.
(a) A digital record registered in a verifiable record system is self-authenticating under [the rules of evidence of this state concerning authentication of records], if accompanied by a written declaration of a qualified person stating:  
  (1) the date and time the record entered the system;  
  (2) the date and time the record was retrieved;  
  (3) that the record was maintained as part of a regularly conducted activity; and  
  (4) that the system’s method of verification was regularly relied upon in such activity.  

(b) A record that is self-authenticating under subsection (a) is admissible in evidence without further foundation, subject to the discretion of the court to require additional proof where circumstances suggest a lack of trustworthiness.  

---

## SECTION 4. BUSINESS RECORDS PRESUMPTION.
A digital record registered in a verifiable record system and accompanied by the declaration described in Section 3(a) is presumed admissible as a record of regularly conducted activity under [the rules of evidence of this state concerning records of regularly conducted activity], unless the source of information or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness.  

---

## SECTION 5. DEFAULT PRESUMPTIONS.
Unless rebutted, the following presumptions apply to a digital record maintained by a verifiable record system:  

(1) A fact or record verified through a valid application of verifiable record technology is authentic.  
(2) The recorded date and time is the date and time the record was added.  
(3) The person identified by the system as the originator is presumed to have created the record.  
(4) A presentation of such a record in a format agreed by the parties is sufficient to demonstrate its contents.  

---

## SECTION 6. LIMITATIONS.
(a) Presumptions under this [act] extend only to the authenticity, integrity, and authorship of the record.  
(b) Presumptions under this [act] do not establish the truth, accuracy, or legal status of the contents of the record.  
(c) A person against whom a record is introduced bears the burden of producing evidence sufficient to support a finding that the record, date, time, or identity is not authentic. The burden of persuasion remains on the proponent of the record.  

---

## SECTION 7. SCOPE OF APPLICATION.
Without limitation, this [act] applies to digital records used to establish:  
(1) contractual parties, terms, execution, effective dates, and status;  
(2) ownership, assignment, negotiation, and transfer of money, property, contracts, instruments, and other legal rights and duties;  
(3) identity, participation, and status in the formation, management, and governance of organizations;  
(4) identity and status in interactions between private parties or between private parties and government; and  
(5) the authenticity or integrity of records relevant to transactions, operations, or communications.  

---

## SECTION 8. RECOGNITION ACROSS JURISDICTIONS.
A digital record authenticated under this [act] in another jurisdiction has the same legal effect and presumptions in this state, unless specific contrary law applies.  

---

## SECTION 9. NO MANDATE OR VALIDATION OF ACTIVITY.
(a) Nothing in this [act] creates an obligation for any person to adopt or implement verifiable record technology.  
(b) Nothing in this [act] validates the legality or authorization of any underlying activity merely because it is recorded or verified through such technology.  

---

## SECTION 10. SEVERABILITY.
If any provision of this [act] or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or applications of this [act] which can be given effect without the invalid provision or application.  

---

# COMMENTARY (for drafters and courts, not statutory text)

1. **Evidence Rule References.** Sections 3 and 4 refer to “rules of evidence of this state concerning authentication of records” and “records of regularly conducted activity.” These bracketed phrases should be aligned by legislative counsel to the state’s evidence code. In states following the Federal Rules of Evidence, these correspond to Rules 902 and 803(6), respectively.  

2. **Technology Neutrality.** This act avoids limiting presumptions to “blockchain” and instead covers any cryptographically verifiable system (e.g., distributed ledgers, append-only logs, zero-knowledge proof–based records).  

3. **Scope.** The act applies to private, commercial, and governmental transactions where records are offered into evidence. It is not intended to address preservation or accessibility of government publications; states may consider adoption of the Uniform Electronic Legal Material Act for that purpose.
